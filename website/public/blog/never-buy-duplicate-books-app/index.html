I bought the same Agatha Christie paperback three times. Twice at the charity shop. Once at a Sunday market. Each time I was absolutely sure I did not own it yet. Each time I got home, found it on the shelf, and groaned.

That is the problem with a book collection that outgrows your memory. You remember the authors you love. You forget the exact titles tucked three rows back on the shelf. So you buy again — and donate the duplicate six months later, still in the bag.

I built SeniorEase Library to fix that. Scan what you own once. Check your collection before you buy. No duplicate books. No wasted euros at the kringloop.

Why duplicate books keep happening

Charity shops and book markets are dangerous when you love reading. Everything costs €1 or €2. You pick up a novel, think that looks familiar, and buy it anyway because you cannot be sure.

At home you have hundreds of books. Maybe thousands if you count the boxes in the attic. No one can remember every spine. A spreadsheet works for a while, but updating it feels like homework — so you stop, and the duplicates start again.

What you need is a never buy duplicate books app that lives on your phone, works at the shop, and does not ask for an account or a monthly fee.

Check your collection before you buy

The habit is simple: scan first, buy second. Before you leave for the market, your shelf is already in the app. At the charity shop, pull out your phone and search by title or author. Already there? Put the book back. Not there? Safe to buy.

Even faster: scan the ISBN barcode on the back cover right where you stand. SeniorEase Library looks it up and tells you in seconds whether it is already in your catalog.
